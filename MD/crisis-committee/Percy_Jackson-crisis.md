ULES OF ROCEDURE
This will be a no-tech committee, and as such use of laptops, phones, and other devices
are strictly forbidden. All directives, JPDs, and backroom notes are to be handwritten on paper
we will provide. Additionally, use of AI, whether for research or for writing directives, is strictly
forbidden, and delegates caught using AI will be disqualified from awards.
The note-cycle will follow standard crisis procedure, with a two-notepad system and
regular crisis updates.
Sponsor and Signatory requirements for directives and JPDs will be decided on the day of
committee. Directives, JPDs, and backroom notes may only be written during committee
sessions and may not be worked on during breaks. Unless specified otherwise, all Rules of
Procedure follow the ROP guide posted on our website.

We understand that our committee centers around religion, and that it is a sensitive topic.

modern and ancient civilizations, and including those of civilizations not mentioned in this

doing research. If a delegate has found information about ancient mythology that is both not
mentioned by Rick Riordan and unpleasant, we ask that they think twice before sharing it with
the committee.

Sensitivity & Harassment Policy:
TritonMUN is committed to fostering a healthy environment for diplomacy and dialogue, and
upholding standards of diversity, equity, and inclusion for all delegates. Harassment or disruptive
behavior of any kind—whether verbal or non-verbal, in or out of committee—is strictly
prohibited. All attendees and staffers should be treated with equal respect, and discrimination on
the basis of race, color, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, nationality, ethnicity, or
physical/mental disability will not be tolerated. This list is not exhaustive, and should not be
treated as such. Please report any instances of harassment to conference staff, either directly
or through the Anonymous Feedback Form.
In addition, we acknowledge that topics discussed in-committee may be politically sensitive,
particularly in Specialized and Crisis Committees. While we understand that delegates may be
immersed in the action and setting of their committee, delegates must maintain decorum and
refrain from inflammatory language and actions, even when doing so may be historically
accurate to one’s character, group, or country. When drafting crisis directives, delegates should
refrain from pursuing any action that explicitly violates human rights, including—but not limited
to—enslavement and genocide. Please refer to your committee’s background guide for
specific sensitivity statements and guidelines, and direct any questions to the committee’s
head chair.
All attendants should observe these policies during the entirety of the conference, even outside
formal debate, including opening/closing ceremonies, “FUNMUN”, etc. Please note that
violation of these policies may result in consequences such as disqualification from awards and
contact towards the delegate’s Advisor. At TritonMUN, our goal has always been focused on the
delegate’s abilities to enjoy and have fun in committee. However, in order to truly enjoy
TritonMUN, delegates must feel safe in committee, which starts with respecting one another.

Plagiarism & Generative AI Policy:
TritonMUN recognizes that Model United Nations provides a space for aspiring leaders to
showcase their work and abilities, and that all delegates wish to be evaluated in a fair and
accurate manner. As such, we expect all delegates to perform with integrity and refrain from
plagiarizing the work of their peers. This includes—but is not limited to—claiming the work of
another delegate as one’s own, including another bloc’s unique clause without prior approval
from the original writer, and replacing another’s work with one’s own (i.e. overwriting).
In addition, delegates are strictly prohibited from utilizing generative AI to produce position
papers, resolutions, and directives, in part or in their entirety. Research conducted using
generative AI risks AI hallucination, generating incorrect information and is strongly
discouraged.
Resolution Work Outside of Committee:
TritonMUN wishes that all delegates use their breaks and downtime as intended: to connect,
relax, and refresh. While not in committee, delegates are strictly prohibited from working on
resolutions and directives. In addition, this means that pre-writing, or preparing resolutions and
directives before the start of the conference, is strictly prohibited.
Technology Policy:
Delegates should expect the use of technology to be limited during committee sessions. Instead
of having a universal technology policy for TritonMUN, such policies are individualized for each
committee and set by their daises. Please refer to each committee’s background guide to
understand their technology policies, and direct any questions to the committee’s head chair.
Repercussive actions will be taken at the discretion of the head chair.

1. The Mist: A mystical force that prevents mortals from seeing gods, monsters, and other
mythical beings.
2. Demi-gods: Children who have one mortal parent and one godly parent, and possess the
powers of their godly power.
3. Olympian: One of the 12 ruling gods of the Greek pantheon.
4. Camp Half-Blood: A summer camp that trains Greek demi-gods in using their powers.
Located on the East Coast of the United States.
5. Thalia’s Tree: A tree that provides a magical barrier around Camp Half-Blood,
preventing monsters of ill intent from entering.
6. Celestial Bronze: A special type of metal that can kill monsters.
7. Mount Olympus: The mountain on which the 12 Olympians rule the world. Has changed
its location from Mount Olympus in Greece to the Empire State Building in New York.
8. Monsters: Creatures from mythology who were distorted by the gods. Monsters reform
in Tartarus when killed.
9. Tartarus: A region in the underworld where monsters reform. Separated from the main
part of the underworld
10. Quests: Actions demi-gods take upon request of the gods. Usually done after a prophecy
is given.
11. Oracles: Mortals with the ability to see the future and see through the mist. Only one
exists at the moment.
12. Drachma: Golden coins used by gods and monsters as currency.
13. Nectar/Ambrosia: The food and drink of the gods. Heals demi-gods upon consumption,
but kills mortals and monsters if consumed.
14. Iris Rainbow: A long-distance communication method used by gods and demi-gods.
Creates a line of communication upon giving a drachma to a rainbow. Kind of like Zoom.
15. River Styx: A river in the underworld. Making a promise on the River Styx creates a
severe punishment for those who break it.

ERCY ACKSONVERSE
Note: This does not take place in the standard Percy Jacksonverse timeline. This is a standalone
story where all characters are coming together. If a delegate wants to get more familiar with the
world of Percy Jackson, we would recommend watching the first season of the television show, or
the first movie.
Generations grew up on the stories of the Ancient Gods, believing them to be nothing
more than myth. However, many moons ago, crowds would worship and fear these deities,
giving them strength in power. In the modern day, only a few know these stories are more than
just bedtime tales, those who are the Half-Blooded children of the Gods, monsters, or the last
believers. People have lost faith in the Greek Gods. With the population of those who used to
worship and believe in them dwindling, the Gods are losing their power. Olympus is weakening
and is fighting to have people believe in them again. The Gods, however, can not fully act on
their free will as they can not break their rules, so they must act through their children, the
demi-gods, to carry out their will.
As the Ancient Gods are already a shadow of themselves at their former power, their
strongest source of strength are their ancient artifacts. Zeus has his lightning bolt, Poseidon
wields his trident, and Hades his helm of darkness, which sustains them in times like these.
While the gods are busy pointing fingers and complaining about their new weakened states, the
minor gods grow unhappy with their mistreatment as their powers remain unaffected as they
were used to little attention. To avoid a conflict between the gods and the devastation from the
resulting war, the demi-gods must come together to find a peaceful resolution.
History of Greek Mythology/Ancient Greece
The civilization of Ancient Greece started out as a collection of small farming villages.
Around this time, the people of Ancient Greece used gods to explain the things around them, and
thus the gods were born. As the farming villages evolved into city-states, they grew more
complex, with markets and governments being created. A side effect of the creation of city-states
12
was that other city-states could raise armies now, and to protect themselves, individual city-states
dedicated themselves to a specific god, leading these gods to protect the cities.
While city-states were protected by the
gods, the Ancient Greeks were still forced to
expand outwards. Since the city-states had an
agriculture-based economy but a finite
amount of land, some people were forced to
leave to find land to grow crops on.
Additionally, while kings were removed,
city-states were ruled by elites who refused to
give common people political power, with
some of these elites being descendents of the gods, encouraging many people to leave to gain
rights. This caused the creation of colonies, city-states that came from, but were independent of,
another city-state.
Unfortunately, the gods were not always capable of helping the Ancient Greeks in times
of war. In one case, the Trojan War, the gods sided with different city-states, escalating the
conflict from one between mortals only to one that also included the gods, making the
destruction of the war greater. Additionally, when Rome was conquering Greece, they adopted a
mythology closely resembling Greek mythology, preventing the Greek gods from having a
military advantage on the Roman gods. With a
centralized Roman Empire accepting new citizens,
they gained power with each Greek city-state they
conquered, while the individual Greek city-states
could not successfully unite against the invading
Romans.
Though conquered, the cities of Greece still survived, along with many aspects of their
culture, including their language. However, the introduction of Roman mythology and, later,
Christianity directly competed with Greek mythology for followers. In spite of the influence
13
Greek culture had on Roman mythology and Christianity, Greek mythology could not withstand
this competition, and Greek mythology slowly faded.
Greek Pantheon
Before the gods and men existed, there were two primordial beings, Gaia and Uranus.
Gaia created the Earth, while Uranus created the sky. They had many children, including the
twelve Titans. Eventually, Gaia and the Titans plotted to kill Uranus, and Kronos, the chief Titan,
killed his father. But before his death, Uranus cursed Kronos to meet the same fate as he did. In
time, Kronos had children of his own, but out of fear of being killed by them, consumed his
children upon birth. His wife, Rhea, managed to hide her last child, Zeus, from Kronos. Zeus
later returned and, pretending to be a servant, served Kronos poisoned wine, causing him to
vomit out his other five children, the first gods. The gods, with alliances with other children of
Uranus, managed to defeat the Titans, with many being thrown into the pit of Tartarus, and
others being subject to other punishment.
With the Titans defeated, the Gods decided to rule over Earth. Zeus, the god of thunder
and the skies, was named king of the gods, and he took the goddess Hera to be the queen of the
gods and his wife. His brothers Poseidon and Hades took control of the sea and underworld,
respectively, and were the most powerful gods aside from Zeus. Eventually, Zeus had children of
his own, and some became members of the twelve
Olympians, the ruling council of the gods. While
Hades refused to become an Olympian, gods and
goddesses like Demeter, goddess of farming, and
Ares, god of war, joined, increasing its strength.
At this time, humanity was far from flourishing.
Seeing the need to bring technology to the humans, the
Titan Prometheus, who sided with the gods against the Titans and created the first humans, gave
them fire against Zeus’s orders, sparking rapid technological development but causing
14
Prometheus to be subject to grave punishment. Additionally, wanting to punish Prometheus’s
brother Epimetheus, Zeus gave a box full of the world’s evils to Pandora, Epimetheus’ wife,
knowing that she’d open it and release evils onto the world. The technological advancement
coupled with the temptations of evil would lead many humans to turn against the gods, with the
gods turning some of them into monsters, for instance Athena turning the human Arachne into a
spider after Arachne’s pride and weaving skill humiliated the gods.
Additionally, some of the gods decided to have affairs with humans, giving rise to
demi-gods, capable of using the power of the gods. Many demi-gods, like Hercules and Perseus,
would slay the monsters the gods created. However, with the gods having affairs with mortals,
sometimes their spouses or other gods grew jealous, with Athena turning Medusa into a monster
after Posiedon had an affair with her in Athena’s temple, and Hera killing all of Zeus’ mistresses
upon learning of their existence. The number of demi-gods and monsters both increased with the
improvement of civilization.
The number of gods also grew during this time. Many Olympians decided to have more
children, and minor gods like Eros were created. Additionally, some demi-gods were able to
achieve immortality, with Hercules being promoted to a lesser god following his twelve trials,
and Dionysus becoming an Olympian upon gaining a large following. However, in spite of the
large number of minor gods, they don’t have a major role in ruling the Earth, with Hercules
being sent to guard an abandoned island, and other gods having no say in the affairs of Mount
Olympus.
However, this is not the case for minor gods in the underworld. Due to the complex
system of governance and large degree of independence from Mount Olympus, Hades finds
many uses for minor gods. Thanatos, the god of death,
is a key minor god that prevents the undead from
leaving the underworld. In addition to employing many
minor gods, Hades is known to employ both monsters
and fallen humans. To get to the underworld, the
ferryman Charon takes paying patrons across the river
Styx, and for souls that make it there, they get judged
15
by the souls of three human judges. Once judged, a soul can be sent to the Fields of Punishment
if they were evil, to the Fields of Asphodel if they were morally neutral, or to Elysium if they
were good. The underworld also connects to Tartarus, though few residents of the underworld
dare go there.
Camp Half-Blood and Demigods
Many Demigods grow up living normal human lives, unknowing that they are half
Olympian. Most are teenagers when they find out about their true parentage and make the trek to
Camp Half-Blood, the home, sanctuary, and training grounds for Demigods. In Camp
Half-Blood, Demigods are housed in Cabins dedicated to their Olympian parent. There are 12
cabins in total, with a cabin for every Olympian, with Hades and minor gods being excluded.
The demi-gods know who their godly parent is, and which cabin they belong to, when they are
“claimed” by their godly parent, a ritual done during dinner time. Children of minor gods and
Hades can not “claim” their children, as they have no cabins, and so all their children go to Cabin
11, Hermes Cabin. Percy Jackson, son of Poseidon, for instance is homed in Cabin 3, also known
as the Poseidon Cabin, and is their lone resident. The cabins of the “Big Three”, along with Hera
Cabin, have few residents as those gods do not have affairs with mortals. Many of the Demigods
stay in camp until the end of their adolescence, completing quests for the gods.
While some demi-gods may make it to Camp Half-Blood on their own, the nature spirits
known as Satyrs typically bring them to Camp Half-Blood, identifying them and protecting them
on their journey to Camp Half-Blood. The lower part of a Satyr’s body is identical to that of a
goat, so they typically use disguises to hide from mortals in addition to being hidden by the Mist.
While Satyrs aren’t the strongest fighters out there, they are really good at detecting demi-gods
and are friendly towards them, allowing them to convince demi-gods to come to Camp
Half-Blood.
Once demi-gods make it to Camp Half-Blood, they begin training their fighting skills to
survive monster attacks. While Camp Half-Blood is protected by Thalia’s Tree, which magically
16
keeps out monsters, the outside world is not and demi-gods frequently get
attacked by monsters. Chiron, the activities director of Camp Half-Blood, trains
demi-gods in using weapons, either personally or through large-scale combat
situations. Demi-gods are provided with Celestial Bronze gear and weapons,
since only Celestial Bronze (or other magical metals) can kill monsters, with
the added benefit of Celestial Bronze being unable to harm mortals.
Usually, at least once a summer, demi-gods are sent on quests to serve the
gods or to help out the camp. The Oracle of Delphi, a mortal gifted with the power of prophecy,
periodically gets cryptic visions of the future, which indicate that the gods have a quest for
demi-gods. What these prophecies mean are hard to understand when given, yet they provide
recommendations for who to send on quests. Demi-gods only go on quests voluntarily and are
given supplies before they head out. Quests are dangerous, and some demi-gods do not return
alive.
Interactions with Mortals
While many mortals exist and some interact with the gods, most don’t see them due to a
mystical force called the Mist. The Mist gives gods, monsters, and mythical events rational
explanations for mortals, and can hide them from view and create false memories if necessary.
While the minor goddess Hecate made the mist after realizing the problems interactions between
mortals and gods could have, they are unable to destroy the Mist, and even the Olympians were
found to be unable to destroy it. However, the Mist can be manipulated by Gods and demi-gods
skilled in magic, allowing Gods to show their true forms to mortals, and allowing demi-gods to
create illusions.
The Mist was strengthened following World War 2, after the gods realized that their
intervention in mortal affairs was causing too much damage. World War 2 was mainly a war
between the children of Zeus and Poseidon on one side, and the children of Hades on the other,
with other demi-gods and even gods taking sides in the war, causing massive damage. Seeing the
damage they and their children were causing, the gods swore to never interact in mortal affairs
again, and Zeus, Poseidon, and Hades, the “Big Three” gods, swore to never again have mortal
children. All of them violated this pact at least once, and each has one surviving demi-god child.
17
While the gods don’t interact with mortal affairs, they frequently form relations with
mortals. Additionally, though most mortals are not able to see the gods, sometimes gods reveal
themselves to mortals, especially when they are forming relationships with mortals. Seeing the
gods allows those mortals to see through the Mist, though the gods do not have relations with
mortals very often, leading only a few mortals to see through the Mist. Mortals that are able to
see through the Mist are able to see monsters, and while monsters can kill mortals, mortals are
unable to harm monsters as they lack the Celestial Bronze weaponry needed to do so. However,
most monsters don’t interact with mortals, and so mortals are generally unharmed by them.
Current Problems
With the weakening of the Olympians, the authority they wield has shrunk. This has
caused monsters to act more boldly against demi-gods, since they know that the assistance the
Olympians can give to their demi-gods children isn’t as much as it was before. Additionally,
minor gods are also more daring in challenging the gods, with Nemesis, the minor god of
vengeance and balance, openly calling the Olympians unjust rulers, and Iris, the minor god of
rainbows and messages, raising the cost of messages from the Olympians.
The weakening of the Olympians has many causes. For one, the expansion of consumer
culture in the modern age has made people less interested in Greek mythology. With easily
accessible and entertaining movies and games featuring characters like Mickey Mouse, Mario,
and Iron Man gaining larger fan bases, Greek Mythology, largely unshown in popular culture,
has been unable to keep up. Another reason for their weakening is the expansion of scientific
knowledge. With individuals like Albert Einstein and Isaac Newton helping further understand
the natural world, the need for Greek Gods to explain the unknown has faded.
Additionally, with individuals like Prometheus and Calypso still being punished, in spite
of thousands of years and good behavior, many within the world of Ancient Greek beings have
questioned the actions of the Olympians. Lesser gods are still excluded from decision-making,
despite outnumbering the Olympians, and many are unhappy with their current status. Even
among demi-gods, some are unhappy with their parents neglecting them, something Olympians
do more compared to the lesser gods.

Case Studies
Culture and its aspects, including but not limited to religion, change over time. Whether a
particular piece of culture survives or dies out depends on many factors: adaptability, popularity,
and accessibility, to name a few. Here we show several cultures and how they adapted to stay
afloat and even thrive in the modern era.
The Spread of Buddhism in East Asia
Originally from India, where the religion of Hinduism was deeply rooted, Buddhism had
trouble spreading. Many tenets of Buddhism, like its denial of class-based religion, went against
the beliefs of Hinduism, and struggled to gain a foothold there. Additionally, members of the
ruling class, including the Brahmins endorsed by Hinduism, opposed Buddhism, denying
Buddhists funding and ridiculing them. In order to survive, Buddhism had to find a more
receptive audience abroad.
Buddhism, through merchants, eventually arrived in China, and adapted to the local
culture in order to gain followers. Many Bodhisattvas, individuals who in the afterlife help others
achieve inner peace, were given
Chinese names, and certain
Bodhisattvas came to represent
material gain, helping it gain
influence with the Chinese merchant
class. Additionally, Buddhism took in
ideas from Daoism, a prominent
Chinese way of thinking, allowing the
ideas of Buddhism to resonate with a
Chinese audience. These adaptations
made Buddhism immensely popular
in China, and by the sixth century
Buddhism was as popular as Daoism.

Buddhism also spread to Korea, though it faced initial opposition from the locals. While
the Korean rulers adopted Buddhism as the official religion, thinking that it would legitimize
their reign, local populations opposed it as they believed in local religions, with local elites in
particular opposing Buddhism as the local religions legitimized their authority. To solve this
issue and spread to local populations, Buddhism and local religions combined, with locals
accepting the Buddha as yet another god, and Buddhists accepting local gods as protectors of the
Buddha. This compromise between religions allowed Buddhism to gain prominence in Korea,
and even today Buddhist temples contain shrines of other gods. Compromising on certain aspects
of Buddhist religion allowed Buddhism to spread in spite of the existing belief system in Korea.
Eventually, the influence of its Chinese and Korean neighbors brought Buddhism to
Japan. In spite of the deeply rooted Shinto religion, Buddhism spread due to its provision of new
religious ideas. Shintoism did not have a clear conception of the afterlife nor a positive outlook
towards death, so Buddhism, by having a positive outlook on dying, filled the niche left by
Shintoism, causing Buddhism to spread without threatening Shintoism. Additionally, seeking to
centralize power, the government found state-sponsored Buddhist temples a useful tool to
increase state control of the populace, and this helped Buddhism spread. By expanding on
previously untouched fields and by gaining governmental support, Buddhism expanded into
Japan, even though it was unable to replace Shintoism.
While today Buddhism has spread far beyond the confines of Asia, its early experiences
show how religions are able to spread. It shows how adapting to local cultures, compromising on
certain beliefs, providing ideas on new subjects and seeking governmental support are all vital
components of spreading religion.
Comebacks in the World of Music: Taylor Swift
Music artists can experience rough patches for a multitude of reasons beyond a natural
decline in popularity and quality, and Taylor Swift is a prime example of this. Taylor Swift’s
career has experienced various ups and downs, with highs of being known as “America’s
20
Sweetheart” to the lows of receiving media scrutiny and having public feuds, which would
culminate in her taking a year-long hiatus from 2016-17. She came back from her hiatus with her
2017 album Reputation, which, like her previous albums, was released to critical acclaim by
fans. For some time following, she had seemingly recovered from her public setback until the
very public battle between Big Machine Records after her contract had expired.
Taylor Swift recorded her first 6 albums with Big Machine Records. Contract disputes
meant that she wouldn’t keep any of the Masters (the original recordings) of her first 6 albums.
Negotiations between businessman Scooter Braun and Big Machine Records would see the
original masters sold to Braun, at the detriment of Taylor, who was never given an opportunity to
purchase her own music. This lack of ownership would see her unable to perform most of her
older catalog and even use her own music on her Netflix documentary, which was released in
2020. This publicized battle between Taylor Swift and Scooter Braun over ownership made
known disputes that were all too common between artists and their music between record labels,
who would write contracts that kept ownership mostly within the record companies as opposed
to the artists.
While Braun held the rights to the original Masters of Taylor’s music, Swift still held the
publishing rights (right to the melodies and lyrics), which meant she could simply re-record her
old albums and own the new versions. She would do this with her first 4 albums, labeling them
as “Taylor’s Version” to critical acclaim, which saw her viewed more as a media sweetheart and
reach popularity similar, if not better, to what she received in the early 2010s. Furthermore, her
“Eras Tour” in 2023, which sought to explore the various eras of her career, created memorable
and immersive fan experiences while launching her into true legend status and cementing her
legacy as one of the greatest singer/songwriters in the 21st century.
Taylor’s Comeback should prove to delegates that a comeback can include taking on an
“us vs them” mentality since people like to root for underdogs. Similarly, rebrandings can help
revitalize the image of gods who may have fallen into irrelevance.
