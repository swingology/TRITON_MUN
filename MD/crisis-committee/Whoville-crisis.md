much information about the town and its lore. For that reason, in this background guide we
wanted to expand upon our vision of Whoville, inventing our own stories of their politics and

THE CANON
Whoville appears in many Dr. Seuss books, such as How the Grinch Stole Christmas and
Horton Hears a Who, in which it is a city contained within a speck of dust. Furthermore, it has a
dense atmosphere which cuts out contact with the outside world, essentially isolating the town.
THE TIMELINE
At the beginning of this year, there was a political shift in Whoville as Ned McDodd
began his term as mayor, a victory over the incumbent candidate Augustus MayWho. McDodd
comes from the Cupcake Party of Whoville, representing the youth who have entered politics
while MayWho and the Muffins were the presiding party, in power for the past couple of years.
With the election, the Cupcakes were excited to prepare for a change, however they did
not expect that change to be a potential crime.
Two weeks after the election, the Chancellor of Who University reported that the original
copy of the university expense tracker had gone missing. According to him, he had been looking
over the university’s budget the night before, and he “could have sworn” he put the journal back
in the safe where it is stored before he left. However, his memory has been failing him recently,
so he believes it’s possible that he is mistaken. The Chancellor says the university is lucky that
they have a digital copy of the tracker, but that he would prefer the original journal to be found
as the digital copy has not been updated with the past months expenditures yet.
One month later, the Chief Medical Officer of Whoville Hospital reported that some
medical files had gone missing. Dr. Wyrd claims that it had been an issue for a couple months
that certain files could not be found, however the hospital had been facing some budget cuts,
which had contributed to administrative issues. For that reason, he thought that the files were
simply “lost in the mess,” but over time as the records were being organized, he realized that
they had a larger problem.
Lastly, at the same time of the aforementioned reports, multiple businesses as well have
found that some items have been lost. At first it was one or two small objects disappearing (like
candy bars from the convenience store), however over time, more things of bigger sizes went
missing in multiple different stores, causing concerns among business owners.
These reports were initially thought of as isolated, however it’s been increasingly clear
that something more devious is at hand, and the people of Whoville are growing more and more
concerned. The Whoville Police Department (WPD) has been facing complaints as the townsfolk
fear the safety of their families and businesses. Civilians are demanding that the WPD step up
and find the culprit sooner rather than later. Additionally, complaints have been lodged at the city
council as larger action is needed, investigations need to be put in place, and there has been no
formal statement from any government officials on the crisis.
With dissent growing louder – articles being written, protests being formed, and distrust
being sowed – Mayor Ned McDodd created a taskforce of important and skilled civilians in
order to begin investigations as well as to provide a centralized course of action that would
assuage everyone.
THE POLITICAL PARTIES OF WHOVILLE
There are two main political parties in Whoville: the Cupcake Coalition and the Muffin
Party. They are not opposed to each other, and oftentimes do agree on many laws and changes,
however they do advocate for different issues, which is why they have differing perspectives on
allocation of resources.
Firstly, the Muffin Party is the older political party in Whoville, with the founders being
the same as the founders of the town. Thus, their focus has been preservation, protection, and
prosperity. Over time, the party has branched into different sections, specialized by topic in
response to the Great Leavening of 1967 (this being a schism long ago, the actual issue of which
no one remembers). However, since the schism, tensions have dissipated: the Muffin Party and
its three affiliated factions (the Blueberries, Lemon Poppies, and Chocolate Chips) have
maintained good relations, though the Muffin Party keeps a certain political edge over the other
three.
Around twenty years ago, the Cupcake Coalition was born in the classrooms of Who
University, with the minds of imaginative students and their supportive professors. These
students wanted to have more room for individual expression, different career opportunities, and
more resources for their out-of-the-box projects – demands that they found difficult under the
Muffin Party’s ruling ideology. Being a new party with no previous affiliations to legitimize
them, they worked hard to create unity within the majority of university students and
departments, and they outreached to businesses with shared interests. Together, they created their
12
TEA (Technology, Education, and Arts) campaign that garnered them multiple city council
positions in consecutive years. With these opportunities, many projects were put in place, the
most notable being the construction of the Whoville Museum and Gallery of Arts, the public
transit system, and the creation of the annual TEA Festival, where anyone can showcase their
research, art, business, or invention to the town.
While these initial projects fared well, bringing both public approval and profit, in later
years, not all Cupcake Coalition projects met the same success. For example, the expansion of
the public transit system into one massive rollercoaster throughout town created a massive
financial deficit to the administration at the time. However, these setbacks were nothing
unexpected for an up-and-coming party, and these failures didn’t detract from the good they were
able to contribute from successful policies.
Eventually, the Cupcake Coalition was able to surpass the Muffin-affiliated parties and
find equal footing with their predecessor, the two parties forming the majority of representative
positions. As financial deficits accumulated over the years, the city council felt a need to select
investments carefully, creating more competition between the Cupcakes and the Muffins. These
tensions rose in the most recent mayoral elections between Ned McDodd (Cupcake) and
Augustus MayWho (Muffin). McDodd, the winner, claims that with his platform he will put his
trust in the bright minds of Whoville, believing that community ingenuity is the way to bring
wealth back to the people. MayWho, as a last remark, stated that he had no issue with Ned
McDodd as a person or a politician, but he simply believed that Whoville needed to focus on
what it does well now and not what it could do well in the future.
WHO UNIVERSITY
Who University is one of the oldest establishments of the town, beginning as a trade
school before expanding to include academics as well. It plays a big cultural role in the town of
Whoville considering that most Whovillians are alumni of the school. The University has a
strong bond with industry and politics in the city. For the former, the university sets up
networking workshops and encourages alumni to return and give advice to students, creating
long-lasting relationships. As for politics, the university is the home of the Cupcake Coalition,
however even prior, Who University students have worked together to enact the political change
that they want to see, hosting protests and writing influential essays and articles.
Recently, the university has put more investment in their art and science departments.
With this aid, the school newspaper, film, theater, etc clubs were able to put out more projects,
connecting with the community and businesses alike, letting art students play a larger role in
Whoville culture than ever before. Additionally, the university has increased the funding towards
research, with projects such as the Outerworld research of Harold Hoovey or the self-peeling
potatoes of Mary Lou LaRue.

INDUSTRY IN WHOVILLE
One of the main industries in Whoville is agriculture, some of the core crops being
beezlenuts, poppatopin, and fluffula tufts. The beezlenut is a sweet nut which is used in a lot of
Whoville desserts, however it produces an oil dangerous to other plants, and has to be grown
carefully (List of Foods in Dr. Seuss Books, n.d.). In the late 1800s, the poison managed to spread
to surrounding crops, causing a famine that lasted for several years. Afterwards, more laws were
enacted outlining beezlenut farming protocols. Additionally, the invention of the hoolabul – an
aerosol that decreases the potency of the oil in adolescent beezlenut plants. Poppatopin is a
corn-like crop that’s also integral to the Whovillian diet, being used in similar ways that maize is
in our world. Poppatopin provides 30% of total calories of the Whoville diet, providing
macronutrients such as protein as well as micronutrients such as Vitamin A
(Goredema-Matongera et al., 2021). It can also be dried, which provides a source of food that
can last through winter (Goredema-Matongera et al., 2021). Lastly, the Fluffula bush resembles
Truffula trees in the Outside World, being a colorful plant made up of soft tufts which is woven
into cloth. It’s a versatile fabric, being both breathable but also resistant. When it is knit and
layered it is a good source of warmth in the winter. Fluffula products can be seen throughout the
clothing industry as well as in household products and furniture. In the past, fluffula fiber was
used to make a thicker kind of paper, however lighter, thinner alternatives have since then taken
precedence.
In addition to agriculture, renewable energy is another important industry for the
functioning of Whoville. All houses in Whoville use solar energy, while the manufacturing and
factory districts use wind turbines. The use of energy in Whoville has been increasing over the
years, which has meant that a bigger push for greater innovation into renewable energy and
general energy production has taken place.
THE GRINCH
Not much is known about the origins of the Grinch, but in old, old records in the
deepest darkest cabinets of Whoville city records are the files on the Grinch’s arrival. The
Grinch is not, in fact, a Who. He is a separate species called a “What,” and he was
displaced as a child, his caretaker at the time accidentally dropping him in Whoville, in
the care of two elderly Who women (The Grinch, n.d.). Despite being bullied in school
(where he was once bullied for being different at a class Christmas party), he was able to
have a generally happy childhood.
In his young adulthood was a mischievous but harmless green-string salesman, later
expanding to selling his wacky inventions. It was a tough business where he was met with a lot
of opposition, but with his quick wit and charm, he was able to stay afloat as an entrepreneur…
until the stock market crash in 2008 where he lost everything. Whoville did nothing to support
him in this time, and he felt a deeper isolation than ever before – being a different species and
all; a deep blue started to grow within his heart, turning his yellow fur to green.
The Grinch now lives on the 3,000 foot high Mount Crumpit with his dog Max, avoiding
all social interaction except for when he decides to torment Whoville, as with Grinch Night –
where a “Sour-Sweet Wind” starts to blow throughout Whoville, triggering a series of events that
calls the Grinch to take his Paraphernalia Wagon to (unsuccessfully) wreak havoc and
nightmares upon the town (Mount Crumpit, n.d.; Halloween Is Grinch Night, n.d.). After Grinch
Night, he has remained a hermit inside his cave-home, scheming and stewing but ultimately
avoiding Whoville. [Additionally, at the time of this committee, the events of How the Grinch
Stole Christmas have not happened yet.]
